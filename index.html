My work sits at the intersection of observational astrophysics and machine learning: building graph neural networks to classify and sub-type supernovae, running radiative-transfer and hydrodynamical simulations to understand how stars live and die, and extracting cosmological signal from CMB and pulsar-timing datasets. I've used Bayesian neural networks to characterize black hole accretion flows, validated exoplanet candidates against Gaia data, and applied U-Net and ensemble models to classify sea-ice and atmospheric aerosols from satellite radar. Across these projects I move between Python, C++, and FORTRAN, and between tools like CMFGEN, GADGET, MESA, PyTorch, and PyTorch Geometric — work that has so far produced three journal papers, including one in Nature Astronomy.
