My work sits at the intersection of observational astrophysics and machine learning: building graph neural networks to classify and sub-type supernovae, running radiative-transfer and hydrodynamical simulations to understand how stars live and die, and extracting cosmological signal from CMB and pulsar-timing datasets. Across these projects I move between Python, C++, and FORTRAN, and between tools like CMFGEN, GADGET, MESA, PyTorch, and PyTorch Geometric — work that has so far produced three journal papers, including one in Nature Astronomy.

Machine Learning for Supernovae 4 Projects
Sky surveys like ZTF and LSST flag millions of changing objects every night, and there is never enough telescope time to follow all of them up. I build graph neural networks that learn from supernova observations, so the rare and interesting explosions can be picked out quickly and followed while they are still bright.

Projects

Real-time supernova classification: a Fink science module

Which of tonight's alerts is worth a telescope?
In development · Apr 2026 – present

A graph neural network, built with PyTorch Geometric, that classifies supernovae from photometry on the ZTF alert stream. It is developed as a Fink science module with the ZTF Observational Facility at Caltech and IIA, and is designed to attach to the LSST alert stream as well.

PyTorch GeometricFinkZTFLSST

Sub-classifying Type Iax supernovae with a GNN

Type Iax supernovae come in a wide range of brightness. Is there a pattern?
Published · Nature Astronomy 2026 (co-author)

A graph neural network that estimates an empirical relation for Type Iax supernovae and clusters their spectra into sub-classes. It is based on optical observations from the 2m Himalayan Chandra Telescope and the GROWTH-India Telescope, and it grew out of my master’s thesis.

Graph neural networksSpectral clustering2m HCTGROWTH-India

From 2D observations to a 3D supernova environment

We see a supernova as a flat image. What does its surroundings look like in 3D?
Completed · validated against CMFGEN

An invertible deep learning model that reconstructs the three-dimensional environment of a supernova from two-dimensional data. I checked it against CMFGEN radiative-transfer models, the same code I used for Type IIP spectroscopy at DESY.

Invertible networksCMFGENRadiative transfer

SN 2025wny: the first strongly lensed Type I superluminous supernova

A supernova magnified by a galaxy in front of it, seen at z = 2.01.
Ongoing · IIA & ZTF/Caltech

Spectroscopic confirmation and analysis of SN 2025wny, together with deep-learning photometric classification aimed at catching superluminous supernovae early in the alert stream.
